\begin{abstract}
Property-based testing requires generators that efficiently produce diverse
inputs satisfying a given predicate.
Existing techniques automate generator construction, but their fixed algorithms
may fail to produce efficient generators for some predicates.
Agents can address this limitation by reasoning about predicate semantics to
select generation strategies or reformulate predicates into logically equivalent
forms that admit more efficient generators.
Proving soundness and completeness in an interactive theorem prover can guarantee
the correctness of agent-constructed generators, but the additional proof effort
makes construction harder to scale as specifications and properties grow more
complex.
This paper proposes an approach in which an agent rewrites the predicate in an
intermediate representation and proves each step equivalent, while the
generator and its correctness proofs are derived mechanically from the final
form.
We evaluate the approach through experiments on a toolchain built on top of
SpecTec, a domain-specific language for specifying programming languages.
\end{abstract}
